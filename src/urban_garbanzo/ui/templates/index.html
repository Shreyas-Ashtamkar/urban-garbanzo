Prompt evaluation

Know where a prompt will fail before the model tells you the expensive way.

urban-garbanzo helps teams review prompts as working instructions, not just text blobs. You can inspect whether the ask is clear, bounded, specific, dense enough to be useful, and safe enough to avoid inviting low-trust output.

Instead of bouncing between vague rewrites and repeated model runs, the editor gives you a tighter loop: draft, score, understand the rationale, and improve the prompt with a more obvious next step.

Workflow

A clearer prompt-review loop, shown as a sequence instead of a pile of features.

The point is not to collect scores for their own sake. The point is to move from rough prompt to reliable prompt with an obvious path between each step.

01

Draft the instruction with intent

Start with the real task, target audience, expected output shape, and any hard constraints. This makes the first evaluation meaningful instead of cosmetic.

02

Inspect how the prompt is likely to behave

Read the scoring dimensions as a map of weaknesses. Low clarity, low density, and elevated risk usually point to a specific revision problem, not a generic “bad prompt.”

03

Use the rationale to tighten the next version

Add boundaries, force output structure, reduce repetition, and make success criteria more explicit until the prompt stops leaving critical decisions to model guesswork.

Inside the review

How prompts earn stronger scores Specificity over suggestion

Prompts usually score better when they stop implying the desired outcome and start naming it. That means identifying the audience, the format, the priority order, the constraints, and the conditions under which the model should avoid inventing details.

In practice, this helps with everything from launch checklists to incident reviews to customer communication prompts. The product is useful when a team already knows the task but wants the instruction to behave more reliably across repeated use.

Why rationale matters Actionable critique beats a naked score

A score without explanation rarely tells you what to change. A rationale gives you a more exact next move: add decision criteria, bound the length, keep facts fixed, specify the end format, or reduce duplicate phrasing that muddies the instruction.

What the dimensions reveal Each metric points to a different failure mode
  • Clarity: the task is too implied, not sufficiently stated.
  • Correctness: the prompt may lead the model into invalid or unstable output.
  • Density: either too thin to act on or too padded to parse efficiently.
  • Risk: the instruction invites invention where evidence or grounding is thin.
  • Redundancy: repeated phrasing lowers signal-to-noise and blurs priorities.

Who it helps

Built for teams that need better prompts, not more prompt theater.

This is most useful where prompts are part of production work: writing, analysis, support, planning, QA, operations, and internal AI tooling.

Product, ops, and AI platform teams

Teams often know that a prompt feels weak but cannot quickly explain why. That slows review, makes handoffs fuzzy, and encourages repeated direct testing instead of focused revision.

urban-garbanzo gives those teams a common critique frame. It becomes easier to say “this is missing output structure” or “this still invites unsupported assumptions” than to rely on subjective taste.
